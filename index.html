The energy markets

Energy prices are set by the most expensive source needed to meet demand

This is the merit order principle. Power plants are stacked from cheapest to most expensive. The last plant needed to meet demand sets the price for everyone. Use the sliders below to explore how different factors shift the price.

How the merit order works

Power plants are dispatched in order of their marginal cost — the cost of producing one additional unit of electricity.

Renewables like wind and solar have near-zero marginal costs, so they are dispatched first. Then nuclear, followed by coal and gas.

The electricity price is set by the last plant needed to meet demand — the so-called marginal producer. This means all producers receive the same price, even if their own costs are much lower.

This is why gas prices often determine the electricity price — even when most power comes from renewables.

What moves the price?
